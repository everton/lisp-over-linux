* 0. What Morphic is, in one page

Morphic is the user interface of Self (Maloney and Smith, /Directness and Liveness
in the Morphic User Interface Construction Environment/, UIST 1995), carried into
Squeak by John Maloney and still the ground Pharo stands on. Its ideas, in the
vocabulary this doc will reuse:

- *A morph* is an object that is on screen. It has *bounds* (a rectangle), an
  *owner* and *submorphs* (a tree), and it answers two kinds of message: /draw
  yourself/ and /here is an event/. A button, a text editor, a window and a sprite
  are all morphs — the difference is only which methods they override.
- *The World* is the root morph: the whole screen. Everything visible is somewhere
  in its tree, and the order of its children is the *z-order* — later is on top.
- *The Hand* is the pointer, as a morph. It carries what you are dragging: picking
  a morph up moves it from its owner into the Hand, dropping it moves it into
  whatever morph is under the Hand.
- *Damage.* A morph that changes says which rectangle of the screen is now wrong
  ("invalidate"); once per cycle the World redraws only those rectangles, and in
  each one only the morphs that overlap it. This is what makes overlapping windows
  affordable.
- *Stepping.* A morph may ask to be stepped every N milliseconds; that is how
  things move on their own (liveness), with no thread per morph.
- *The halo.* A ring of handles around any morph — move, resize, inspect, delete,
  duplicate — on any morph, including ones nobody wrote handles for. It is
  /directness/: there is no separate "edit mode" for the UI; the running UI is the
  thing you edit.

The slogan the project already uses, from [[file:code-browser.org][code-browser.org]] §1: /Smalltalk was never an
X application; Smalltalk was the window system./ Phase 7 is that sentence taken
literally for this machine.

* 1. Where we stand — what already exists, checked

The tiled shell was designed so that this step would not be a rewrite (§4¾ of
[[file:code-browser.org][code-browser.org]]: "a trail simply /becomes/ a draggable window if/when Morphic
arrives — nothing above is rewritten"). What that leaves us with today:

And the measured budget it has to fit in ([[file:framebuffer.org][framebuffer.org]], tag #24, the laptop at
1920×1200): a whole frame is *5.04 ms* — =draw-browser= 3.92 ms, =present-fb= 1.12
ms — and a pointer move 6 µs. So a full redraw is already ~200 a second; damage
repair is about keeping a /window drag/ cheap and making room for many live morphs,
not about rescuing a slow renderer.

* 2. The decision this doc proposes

*Morphs are CLOS objects; the World owns the one event loop; the tiled browser
becomes the first morph, unchanged inside.* Concretely:

1. A =morph= class (=standard-class=, so the model layer, the Inspector, =all-instances=
   and the halo already understand it) with bounds, owner, submorphs, and three
   generic functions: =draw-morph=, =handle-event=, =step-morph=.
2. A =world= morph that *replaces* the body of =run-browser-fb='s loop: poll, turn
   records into events, dispatch, step, repair damage, present.
3. The whole current shell becomes *one* morph, =shell-morph=, whose =draw-morph=
   calls =draw-browser= into its bounds and whose =handle-event= calls
   =browser-key= / =browser-click= / =browser-drag=. Day one, the screen is a World
   with one full-screen child, and every =check-*.lisp= must pass unchanged.
4. From there, windows: a trail can be /torn off/ into a =trail-window= morph that
   draws its current pane with the same =draw-pane-body= — the "trail becomes a
   window" promise — and overlapping windows arrive with z-order and damage.

What it deliberately is *not*: a compositor for other processes' pixels
([[file:processes.org][processes.org]] declines that, with reasons), a retained scene graph of every glyph,
or a rewrite of =present=. =present= stays a pure =subject → view=; morphs are the
shell's side of "content proposes, the shell disposes".

* 3. The objects

** 3.1 =morph=

#+begin_src lisp
;; proposal — not in the tree
(defclass morph ()
  ((bounds   :initarg :bounds :accessor morph-bounds)   ; (x y w h), in WORLD pixels
   (owner    :initform nil    :accessor morph-owner)
   (children :initform '()    :accessor morph-children) ; back to front: last is on top
   (step-ms  :initform nil    :accessor morph-step-ms)  ; NIL = never stepped
   (subject  :initarg :subject :initform nil :accessor morph-subject))) ; what it shows, if anything

(defgeneric draw-morph (morph canvas clip))   ; paint yourself; CLIP is the damaged rect
(defgeneric handle-event (morph event))       ; return T if consumed, NIL to let it bubble
(defgeneric step-morph (morph now-ms))        ; liveness; call INVALIDATE if you changed
(defgeneric morph-affordances (morph))        ; halo handles: defaults from COMMANDS-FOR
#+end_src

Two choices worth defending:

- *World coordinates, not owner-relative.* Squeak's morphs keep absolute bounds
  too; it makes hit-testing and damage a matter of comparing rectangles, with no
  transform stack. Moving a window moves its subtree (one loop over descendants).
- *=subject= is a slot, not a subclass.* A morph that shows a subject keeps the
  subject and its =PANE=; the morph is the frame around it. That keeps the halo's
  existing layers ("the pane", "what it shows", "the code that draws it") intact.

** 3.2 =world= and =hand=

The =world= is a morph the size of the framebuffer, plus the loop's state: the
damage list, the keyboard focus, the stepping list, and the =hand=. The =hand= is a
morph that is *not* drawn into the canvas — the §9.5 contract of [[file:framebuffer.org][framebuffer.org]]
stays: the arrow is composited as a patch after the frame, so a bare pointer move
still costs microseconds and =check-pointer.lisp= still holds. What the hand adds is
=hand-carrying=: the morph being dragged, and its grab offset.

** 3.3 Events

One event struct, built from what the loop already decodes:

| kind        | from                                         | carries                          |
|-------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------|
| =:key=      | the tty / the keyboard's evdev               | key, modifier state (=super-p= …) |
| =:down/:up= | evdev buttons, the pad's emulated ones       | button, x, y, click count         |
| =:move=     | evdev =REL/ABS=                              | x, y, buttons held                |
| =:wheel=    | =REL_WHEEL=                                  | notches, x, y                     |
| =:service=  | an =*fb-services*= fd woke the poll          | which service                     |

Dispatch: keys go to the *focus* morph and bubble up through owners; pointer events
go to the *topmost morph under the point* (=morph-at=, a back-to-front walk) and
bubble likewise. A morph that consumes nothing costs nothing. =:service= events go to
whoever registered the service, which is how web panes and terminals keep working.

* 4. Drawing and damage

** 4.1 The canvas needs a clip rectangle

Today every primitive in [[file:../poc/canvas.lisp][canvas.lisp]] clips *to the canvas* ("Every one of these
clips to the canvas, so callers never have to check"). Damage repair needs it to
clip to /a rect inside/ the canvas, or a window repainting itself would scribble over
the window above it. Proposal: clip slots on =canvas= (default: the whole canvas) and
a =with-clip= macro; =fill-rect=, =draw-rect=, =draw-line=, =draw-poly=, =draw-circle=,
=blit-rgba= and the glyph blit intersect with it. It is the one change below the shell, and the only one that touches every
pixel path — so it ships first, alone, with =poc/render-frame.lisp= frames compared
before/after (they must be identical).

** 4.2 The cycle

#+begin_example
poll → events → dispatch → step due morphs → damage list → merge rects
     → for each rect: with-clip, draw every morph overlapping it, back to front
     → present-fb-rect each rect → put-cursor
#+end_example

- =invalidate morph [rect]= pushes a rect (default: the morph's bounds) onto the
  world's damage list. Moving a morph invalidates its old and its new bounds.
- *Merging*: rects that overlap, or whose union is not much bigger than their sum,
  merge; above a threshold (say half the screen) the cycle just repairs the whole
  screen, which at 5 ms is never wrong, only wasteful.
- *The legacy shell morph* can't yet say /which/ of its parts changed, so at first
  it invalidates its whole bounds on any state change — i.e. exactly today's
  behaviour. Finer damage inside it (the terminal's dirty rows already do this) can
  come later, one view at a time, without changing the protocol.

** 4.3 Opaque window drag, costed

Dragging a 900×600 window by 10 px repairs two rects of ~900×610 ≈ 1.1 MP, against
2.3 MP for the whole 1920×1200 screen — about half a frame, ~2.5 ms on the laptop,
dominated by redrawing what the window uncovered. Comfortably under one 60 Hz frame
(16.7 ms). An outline drag (draw only the frame while moving, the real window on
release) is the fallback if a heavy view underneath — a web pane's screencast — makes
it worse. *Decided: opaque* (§9), the common choice today.

* 5. Windows

A =trail-window= is the "trail becomes a window" of §4¾:

- *Contents:* one trail — its breadcrumbs and its current pane, drawn by the same
  =draw-crumb= / =draw-pane-body= the tiled shell uses, into the window's bounds.
  No view code changes; only the rectangle they are given.
- *Frame:* a title bar (the trail's title, the view-kind badge and tint
  =draw-panel= already draws), a close box like the tab strip's, a resize corner.
- *Tearing off:* a key (§6) or dragging a tab out of the tab strip makes the trail a
  window; dropping it back on the strip re-tiles it. The tiled shell stays the
  /default arrangement/: until you tear something off, nothing on screen differs
  from today.
- *Fonts:* a window's title bar and every metric inside it ask =char-w= / =line-h= at
  draw time ([[file:fonts.org][fonts.org]]'s rule). A resize or a font change only changes the rect.

Modal overlays — the Spotter, the chooser, the halo menu, the clipboard manager,
the cheatsheet — become morphs on top of the World instead of nested loops (§7,
step 4). That is the change that makes them stop freezing everything under them: a
terminal keeps scrolling behind an open Spotter.

* 6. Keyboard first, still

The shell is keyboard-first and Morphic must not undo that. Every window operation
gets a key; the mouse is an accelerant. Proposed (=SuperL-g= is free today — checked
against =browser-key='s Super branch in [[file:../poc/shell.lisp::defun browser-key][shell.lisp]]):

| keys                    | does                                                          |
|-------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| =SuperL-g=              | "grab": tear the current trail off into a window / re-tile it |
| =SuperL-g= then arrows  | move the focused window (Shift: by a larger step)             |
| =SuperL-g= then =C-=arrows | resize it                                                  |
| =SuperL-Tab= (existing) | cycles trails — and therefore windows, raising each           |
| =SuperL-w= (existing)   | closes the trail — and its window                             |

Every new key goes into the =SuperL-?= cheatsheet ([[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun draw-cheatsheet][=*cheatsheet*=]] in fb-browser.lisp)
in the same commit that binds it — a "Windows" group, plus the halo's new handles.

The halo gains *move* and *resize* handles on window morphs; its existing handles
(Inspect, Code, Senders) keep working, and Inspect on a window shows the =morph=
instance — its bounds as editable fields, so =C-c C-c= on =bounds= moves it. That is
the Self/Squeak directness, reached through the inspector we already have.

* 7. The plan, in steps that each leave a working system

Each step is shippable on its own, ends with every existing =check-*.lisp= green,
and adds its own test.

0. *Clip rect in the canvas* (§4.1). Host: =poc/render-frame.lisp= frames are
   byte-identical before/after; a new host test draws through a clip.
1. *The World with one morph.* =morph=, =world=, =hand=, events, the cycle;
   =shell-morph= wraps the whole browser. No visible change. Guest: every existing
   check, plus the halo listing =SHELL-MORPH= as the outermost layer.
2. *Windows.* =trail-window=, tear-off / re-tile, z-order, damage, raise on focus,
   =SuperL-g=. Guest test (=check-morphic.lisp=, proposed): QMP drags a window with
   the real pointer and asserts bounds over the net REPL, screendump shows the
   uncovered region repaired; =draw-*= call counts prove only damaged morphs drew.
3. *Halo handles that act on morphs:* move, resize, raise, close, and Inspect on the
   morph instance.
4. *Overlays as morphs.* The six =run-*-fb= modal loops and the value prompt become
   morphs in the one loop. The largest step by line count, done one overlay at a
   time; each keeps its keys.
5. *Stepping.* The Stage's sprites, run panes, and worker liveness ([[file:processes.org][processes.org]])
   move onto =step-morph= / =:service= events, replacing their special cases in the
   loop.

Steps 0–2 are the core — after them the browser /is/ the first window on a Lisp
window system. *Decided (§9): all six steps are in scope, halo included*, built in
the order above.

* 8. Costs and risks, honestly

- *Every input path changes in step 1.* The input loop is the most tested code here
  (=check-input=, =check-pointer=, =check-scroll=, =check-clipboard=, =check-terminal=,
  =check-web=). That is the reason step 1 changes /nothing visible/: the suite is
  the proof.
- *Hit maps survive inside the shell morph.* =*list-rows*= & co. are not removed in
  steps 1–2; they become the shell morph's private business. Replacing them with
  submorphs is optional later work, not a precondition.
- *Web panes are fds plus a screencast.* A web pane inside a window must clip its
  JPEG blit to the window and to damage; the clip rect (step 0) covers it.
- *The X11 dev backend* ([[file:x11.org][x11.org]], =poc/run-browser.lisp=) draws the same canvas, so
  it inherits the World for free as long as the cycle stays backend-blind — the
  same discipline =present-fb= vs =PutImage= already keeps.
- *Session persistence.* A desk file is being built ([[file:session-persistence.org][session-persistence.org]]);
  once windows exist, the desk should record each window's bounds and z-order. Not a
  dependency, a follow-up.

*Why opaque is "the common one".* The user asked which is more usual, so for the
record: every mainstream desktop drags opaque by default today — macOS always has,
Windows has shown window contents while dragging since the late 1990s (it is the
"Show window contents while dragging" option, on by default), and the compositing
X11/Wayland desktops (GNOME, KDE) do too. Outline dragging is what slow machines
did — early X window managers such as twm, Windows 95 on modest hardware — because
repainting what the window uncovered was too expensive. Squeak's Morphic drags the
morph itself. At ~2.5 ms per step (§4.3) we are on the cheap side of that trade.
These are general facts about those systems, not checked here against their source.

* 10. Status, step by step

** Step 0 — the clip rectangle: BUILT (2026-10-16)

[[file:../poc/canvas.lisp::defmacro with-clip][=with-clip=]] in [[file:../poc/canvas.lisp][canvas.lisp]]. The canvas carries four more slots — the clip's
edges, half-open (=x0 <= x < x1=), because that is the form a fill loop wants — and a
fresh canvas clips to itself, so a caller that never says =with-clip= gets exactly
the old behaviour. Clips *nest by intersection* (a body cannot widen the clip it was
given) and are put back by =unwind-protect=, so a debugger restart out of a drawing
function cannot leave the screen half-clipped.

Every pixel writer honours it — the plan's list (=fill-rect=, =draw-rect=, =draw-line=,
=draw-poly=, =draw-circle=, =blit-rgba=, both glyph blitters) and the ones a =grep= for
=canvas-pixels= found /outside/ canvas.lisp, which the plan had not listed:

| writer                  | where                         | now                                                     |
|-------------------------+-------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------|
| the modal scrim         | [[file:../poc/widgets.lisp::defun scrim][=scrim=]], widgets.lisp         | loops over the clip's rows only                         |
| a web page's frame      | [[file:../initramfs/web.lisp::defun web-blit][=web-blit=]], web.lisp           | through the new =blit-pixels=                           |
| an image pane's picture | [[file:../poc/shell.lisp::defun draw-image-body][=draw-image-body=]], shell.lisp  | through =blit-pixels= with =:mask #xFFFFFF=             |
| the halo's spotlight    | [[file:../poc/shell.lisp::defun spotlight][=spotlight=]], shell.lisp        | unchanged: it scrims (clipped) and puts back rows it copied itself — rewriting a pixel's own old value is harmless |
| the pointer patch       | [[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun put-cursor][=put-cursor=]], fb-browser.lisp | unchanged: it /reads/ the canvas into a scratch canvas of its own |

So there is now one rule for code outside canvas.lisp: *copy pixel vectors with
=blit-pixels= (or =blit-canvas=), never by indexing =canvas-pixels=* — a writer that
indexes the vector itself escapes the clip. =blit-pixels= is a =REPLACE= per row, the
same copy =web-blit= always did, so it costs nothing.

One small speed-up rides along, because damage repair draws a whole frame's text
through a narrow clip: a =draw-string= whose line lies wholly above or below the clip
returns its pen at once without touching a glyph. The margin is a full cell either
side, so no glyph a clip would have shown is skipped.

*Proof.* [[file:../poc/clip-test.lisp][poc/clip-test.lisp]] (34 checks): each writer drawn once unclipped and once
through an awkward clip rect must agree pixel for pixel inside the rect and leave
the background untouched outside it (with a "the drawing really reached the rect"
guard, so no check passes vacuously); clips nest, survive a non-local exit, and an
empty one draws nothing; and twelve clipped tiles of a picture reproduce the picture.
=render-frame.lisp= frames before and after, with the tray's clock and battery frozen
so that two runs of the /same/ tree are byte-identical: 9 of 13 subjects identical
(=matrix:COLLIDE=, =stage:120=, =defn:draw-alien=, =workspace=, the Spotter, the halo,
an onion, =workspace= at 800×500 and 12 px, =package:CL-USER= drilled at 30 px); the
other 4 (=modules=, =class:STREAM=, =tour=, =modules= in the bitmap font) differ only
where they show the /code itself/ — a definition count, a =file:line=, the live
instances' addresses — which the change necessarily moved. Every regression check
passed on the step-0 image.

*Not a finding, recorded so nobody repeats it:* =render-frame.lisp= did not load at
all on this tree before the step began (=WATCH-LOCKS is undefined=). That was the
session-log work of the same day: =agent.lisp= names its locks to the stall watcher
at load time, and the harness did not load =poc/host-logging.lisp=. Fixed in the
harness; nothing to do with the clip. Likewise =poc/web-test.lisp= fails "a frame
arrived and was decoded" on this host with or without the clip (checked on the
untouched tree) — a host matter, not this step.

** Step 1 — the World with one morph: BUILT (2026-10-16)

Two files, split the way the shell and the toolkit are split:

- [[file:../poc/morph.lisp][poc/morph.lisp]], package =lol.morph=, knows nothing about the browser: the [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defclass morph ()][=morph=]]
  class (bounds, owner, children, =step-ms=, =subject=, and a =draws= counter the
  tests read), the [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defclass world (morph)][=world=]] and the [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defclass hand (morph)][=hand=]], the event struct of §3.3, rectangles,
  [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun invalidate][=invalidate=]], [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun merge-damage][=merge-damage=]], [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun world-repair][=world-repair=]] and [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun world-dispatch][=world-dispatch=]]. Generic
  functions =draw-morph=, =handle-event=, =step-morph=, =morph-affordances= as
  designed, plus three the build needed: =morph-opaque-p= (see the repair rule
  below), =drop-morph= (the hand let go) and =world-after-dispatch= (the world's
  chance to tidy up after any event).
- [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp][poc/morphic.lisp]], in =CL-USER= beside the shell, is the browser's side: the
  [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defclass desk (lol.morph:world)][=desk=]] (the world the environment runs in, =*world*=) and [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defclass shell-morph (lol.morph:morph)][=shell-morph=]], whose
  =draw-morph= is =draw-browser= and whose =handle-event= is the old loop's routing
  — =browser-key=, =browser-click=, =browser-drag=, =browser-drag-end=,
  =browser-scroll=, the menus — moved, not rewritten.

The guest's loop ([[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun run-browser-fb][=run-browser-fb=]]) now only /decodes/: [[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun input-events][=input-events=]] is the old
decoding, step for step and in the same order (the keyboard's evdev, then the
console, then the pointers), producing events instead of acting; the world routes
them; the loop sends what =world-repair= drew. The old =dirty= flag is gone: a state
change /damages/ the shell morph instead, and the agent's tick, a run pane, a
service's =:redraw= and a stage tick do so through one function,
[[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun environment-changed][=environment-changed=]] — the five special cases step 5 will turn into stepping. The
pointer contract of [[file:framebuffer.org][framebuffer.org]] §9.5 is untouched (the hand is not drawn; a bare
move damages nothing); the stall watcher's =note-main-idle= / =note-main-busy= live
in =poll-input-once=, which the loop still calls; =*fb-services*= still run every
turn. The X11 host loop ([[file:../poc/run-browser.lisp][run-browser.lisp]]) and the frame renderer ([[file:../poc/render-frame.lisp][render-frame.lisp]],
whose =-k= script now goes through =world-dispatch= and gained a
=drag:X1,Y1,X2,Y2= token) run the same world.

*Two rules the design left open, decided in the build:*

- *Repair starts at the topmost opaque morph that covers the rect.* Every morph
  overlapping a damaged rect is drawn back to front — except that nothing under an
  opaque morph covering the whole rect can show, so the walk starts there. With
  windows (step 2) that is what makes a moved window cost a copy rather than a
  repaint of the shell beneath it.
- *Damage rects merge only when they overlap.* The design said "or whose union is
  not much bigger than their sum"; but a window moved down by a few pixels leaves a
  thin strip /touching/ its new bounds, and their union is a window and a half of
  shell to redraw, where apart they are a strip of shell plus a window-sized copy.
  More than 16 rects, or one covering the screen, become one full-screen repair.

*Proof.* Every regression check passed on the step-1 image, and [[file:../check-morphic.lisp][check-morphic.lisp]]'s
section A (9 checks): the environment is a desk whose only child is the shell morph,
covering the screen and holding the focus; a bare pointer move repairs nothing (the
shell's =draws= counter does not move); a key repairs it once, as one full-screen
rect; the halo's outermost layer is the =SHELL-MORPH= instance, both asked of
=halo-layers= and by walking a real halo (QEMU's middle button, then =Up=) all the way
out. =render-frame.lisp= frames are unchanged from step 0 for the same 9 subjects as
there; the halo frame differs by its caption, which now lists one more layer.

*Found on the way, not a design question:* the first image died at its first wake —
=invalid number of arguments: 2= — because =world-repair= with nothing to repair
returned one value where the loop's =multiple-value-call= expected two. It now
returns =(values nil nil)=. Recorded because the symptom was "every check fails"
with nothing on the console: the environment's =handler-case= drops to the menu,
and a diskless guest has no session log; the condition was caught by rebinding
=log-condition= over the net REPL and re-entering with =b=.

** Step 2 — windows: BUILT (2026-10-16)

The browser is now the first window of a Lisp window system. All of it is in
[[file:../poc/morphic.lisp][poc/morphic.lisp]]; the shell gained four small hooks, each a no-op without windows
(=*trail-windowed-p*=, =*status-pane*=, =*backdrop-fn*=, =*halo-outer-layers*=), and
=draw-browser= was split so that its middle — breadcrumbs plus the expanded pane —
is [[file:../poc/shell.lisp::defun draw-trail-area][=draw-trail-area=]], callable into any rect's worth of canvas. The split was proven
byte-identical: every =render-frame.lisp= subject renders as before.

*** What a window is

- *A trail, still in =*trails*=.* Tearing a trail off does not take it out of the
  list: a trail is either in the tab strip or in a window, and everything that walks
  =*trails*= — the terminal sweep that hangs up a closed terminal, the web sweep, the
  desk file, the "unsaved files?" question at =SuperL-q=, the agent — keeps seeing it
  with no change. The window finds its trail by the trail's *root pane*, the one thing
  that never changes identity (drilling replaces the list, never its first pane). The
  tab strip simply skips windowed trails; each keeps its number, so =SuperL-N= still
  reaches it, and the title bar shows that number.
- *Focus is =*active*=, as it always was.* The focused trail is the focused thing:
  when it is a window's, that window has the keyboard; when it is a tiled one, the
  shell has. So =SuperL-Tab= and =SuperL-1..9= reach windows — and raise them — with
  no code of their own. After every event [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun desk-sync][=desk-sync=]] makes the world agree with
  the trails: a window whose trail closed goes, the focus follows =*active*=, the
  window that gets it is raised, a font change redraws everything, and a modal loop
  that drew whole frames gets the whole screen repaired.
- *A backing store, and its own hit maps.* The design said a window would draw its
  pane with =draw-pane-body= "into the window's bounds". The shell's drawing code,
  though, lays out from fixed offsets at 0,0 and records what it drew where in a
  dozen specials (=*list-rows*=, =*content-top*=, =*crumb-rects*=, …) that its click
  handlers read back. So each window draws into *a canvas of its own size*, at 0,0,
  and keeps *its own values of those specials* ([[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun call-with-window-context][=call-with-window-context=]], a =progv=
  over the list in =+window-specials+=); its events arrive in its own coordinates.
  The browser code is called unchanged — =browser-key=, =browser-click= — and never
  learns it is in a window. The repair then copies the backing store onto the screen
  (=blit-canvas=, clipped). This deviates from Squeak, which draws a morph straight
  onto the screen, and it buys the opaque drag almost for free: a window that moves is
  /copied/, never redrawn.
- *A minimal title bar* (decision 6), in the tab strip's own style: the trail's kind
  stripe, its number, its title (the accent when focused), a close box =×= at the
  right; a one-pixel outline (accent when focused) and a three-stroke resize grip in
  the bottom-right corner. Every size in it is asked of the font (=char-w=, =line-h=,
  =+bar-h+=), so a bigger font grows the bar and the grip ([[file:fonts.org][fonts.org]]).

*** The keys — all in the =SuperL-?= cheatsheet, group "Windows"

| keys                         | does                                                                                  |
|------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =SuperL-g= on a tiled trail  | tear it off into a window, focused, and enter *grab mode*                             |
| =SuperL-g= on a window       | enter grab mode                                                                       |
| in grab mode: arrows         | move the window one cell (=char-w= across, =line-h= down); Shift: eight              |
| in grab mode: =C-=arrows     | resize it by a cell                                                                   |
| in grab mode: =t= or =SuperL-g= | re-tile: the trail goes back into the tab strip, focused                           |
| in grab mode: =Ret= / =Esc=  | done; any other key also ends the mode and then goes where keys go                   |
| drag a tab down              | tear it off, the window carried by the pointer from there                            |
| drag the title bar           | move (opaque); drop it on the tab strip: re-tile                                     |
| drag the corner grip         | resize                                                                                |
| click =×= / =SuperL-w=       | close the window's trail                                                              |
| =SuperL-Tab=, =SuperL-1..9=  | reach windows too, raising each                                                       |

Grab mode is not a nested loop: it is a slot of the desk (=desk-grab=), and the desk
sees every key before the focus does ([[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun grab-key][=grab-key=]]). Nothing else stops while it is on.

*** What changed against the plan, and why

- *The last tiled trail stays tiled.* =SuperL-g= on the only tab refuses and says
  so in the status line. The tiled shell always shows a trail — every one of its
  drawing paths assumes one — and an empty desktop behind the windows is a design
  question of its own, not a corollary of tearing off.
- *Re-tiling from the keyboard is =SuperL-g= then =t=* (or =SuperL-g= twice), not one
  =SuperL-g=: the first press on a window has to mean "grab it", or a window could
  never be moved without the mouse — the keyboard-first rule of §6.
- *Damage is still coarse where it was coarse.* The shell redraws whole when its state
  changes, as in step 1. A window redraws whole when an event reaches it, and the
  shell then redraws only its status line (where the focused pane's hints and
  messages live) — unless the event changed another trail (a new tab, a closed one),
  when the whole shell does. Things that change without an event (the agent, a run, a
  service, the stage) redraw the shell and every window whose pane is /live/ —
  terminal, web page, stage, run, live worker, conversation ([[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun pane-live-p][=pane-live-p=]]). With a
  window focused the services are told a full redraw is coming, so none paints a fast
  path with the shell's geometry into a pane that is not in the shell; a live window
  is redrawn whenever a service's fd woke the poll ([[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun service-woke][=service-woke=]]). A web page in a
  window gets no hover (the =:motion= service sees the shell's page rect). All of this
  is step 5's to refine: stepping morphs will say exactly what changed.
- *The fast paths survive.* The clock's tick, a terminal's rows and a web frame still
  paint straight into the canvas and send one rect; each now puts back any window
  over that rect first (=recomposite-windows= then — since step 4 the world's
  [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun shell-painted][=shell-painted=]] / =note-painted=; [[file:framebuffer.org][framebuffer.org]] §9.5).
- *The desk file does not record windows yet.* A saved session reopens every trail
  tiled ([[file:session-persistence.org][session-persistence.org]]); bounds and z-order are the follow-up §8 named.
  Leaving to the console and coming back (=b=) likewise re-tiles: the world is made
  fresh per entry, and its windows with it, while the trails survive.
- *Known small gaps, found by reading the code, not fixed:* a click on the
  minibuffer's clear box while a window has the focus is tested against window
  coordinates (the box is the shell's); and the terminal's dirty-row fast path is the
  tiled terminal's only — a terminal in a window repaints whole on each wake. A web
  page /in a window/ is not exercised by a check yet (a terminal is: section C below).

*** Proof

- [[file:../poc/morphic-test.lisp][poc/morphic-test.lisp]] (44 checks, host, against the real shell): after /every/ step
  of a long script — tear off, grab, arrows, Shift, resize, Esc, keys into the window,
  title-bar drag, grip drag, a second window, raise by click, =SuperL-Tab= into and out
  of windows, a drop on the strip, a tab dragged out, the close box, a font change —
  the screen repaired incrementally, rect by rect, equals pixel for pixel the screen
  a full repair draws from scratch; and the economy holds: a move draws no window
  contents, the shell redraws only the uncovered strips, a key in a window redraws the
  window plus the status line. The world records, per repaired rect, which morphs drew
  in it (=:per-rect= in =world-last-repair=) — that is the instrument.
- [[file:../check-morphic.lisp][check-morphic.lisp]] section B (25 checks, in the guest, real input): the same
  operations through evdev — QEMU's pointer and keys — read back over the net REPL,
  plus the screendump: after a real title-bar drag, 24 points in the uncovered strip

  backing store. Section C (4 checks): a terminal torn off into a window — keys reach

- Every regression check passes on the step-2 image (the numbers are in the commit).

** Step 3 — the halo acts on morphs: BUILT (2026-10-16)

The halo already listed each morph under the pointer as a layer. On a *window's*
layer it now offers, first, the handles Morphic's halo exists for —
[[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defmethod lol.morph:morph-affordances ((w trail-window))][=morph-affordances=]] of =trail-window=, put in front of the usual ones by a new
shell hook, =*halo-extra-entries*= (shell.lisp, [[file:../poc/shell.lisp::defun halo-entries][=halo-entries=]]):

| handle  | does                                                                                          |
|---------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Move    | the hand picks the window up where the pointer is: it follows the pointer with *no button held*; a click puts it down |
| Resize  | the same, by the bottom-right corner                                                          |
| Raise   | focus it (its trail becomes =*active*=), so it comes to the top                              |
| Close   | close its trail, as the =×= box                                                               |
| Re-tile | back into the tab strip                                                                       |

Then Inspect, =Code: render-window= and =Class: trail-window=, as before. Move and
Resize turn grab mode on as well ([[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun halo-carry][=halo-carry=]]), so the arrows (=C-=arrows) still
work during the carry and Return or Esc put the window down where it is — the
keyboard-first rule of §6, kept inside a mouse gesture. The world needed one rule for
a carry with no button held: the press that ends it is the hand's, not the window's
under it ([[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun world-dispatch][=world-dispatch=]]); its release drops. And the loop now emits a =:move=
event for a bare pointer move, which it did not before (a bare move had nothing to
tell anyone) — it still damages nothing, so =check-pointer.lisp= holds.

*Inspect with settable bounds.* Inspect on the window shows the =trail-window=
instance; its =BOUNDS= row is settable, and =C-c C-c= with =(list 60 90 700 420)=
moves and resizes the window on screen. The inspector wrote slots with =(setf
slot-value)=, which would have changed the number and nothing on screen; so
[[file:../initramfs/model.lisp::defun inspect-places][=inspect-places=]] now asks a generic function, [[file:../initramfs/model.lisp::defgeneric inspect-slot-setter][=inspect-slot-setter=]] (default:
=(setf slot-value)=), and morphic.lisp gives a morph's =bounds= the setter that
damages: [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun set-morph-bounds][=set-morph-bounds=]], a move then a resize. =owner= and =children= — the tree,
which only =add-morph= / =remove-morph= may change — say "not settable".

*Decided by default, pending the user's review:* the morph handles come /before/
Inspect and Code (Squeak's halo puts its move and resize handles first too, from the
literature); the shell morph gets no affordances — moving the tiled shell means
nothing.

** Step 4 — the overlays are morphs: BUILT (2026-10-16)

Every nested modal loop is gone: =run-spotter-fb=, =run-chooser-fb= (menus, the halo,
a web page's =<select>=), =run-clipboard-fb=, =run-cheatsheet-fb=, =run-frame-source-fb=,
the value prompt (=dbg-prompt-value= / =fb-prompt-value=), the debugger's own loop,
and one the plan did not list — the debugger's ASK view, a loop of its own in
agent-ui.lisp (=run-agent-debugger-view=). Each is an [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defclass overlay (lol.morph:morph)][=overlay=]]: a morph on top of
the desk that the world draws (clipped, back to front, so a scrim dims what is under
it /as it is now/) and that takes every input event while it is up — the desk
routes them to the topmost one ([[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defmethod lol.morph:handle-event ((desk desk) e)][the desk's =handle-event=]]). What is left of each
loop is what it was always about: which key means what (=overlay-event=) and what it
draws (=overlay-draw=, the shell's same =draw-spotter-card=, =draw-context-menu=,
=draw-halo=, =draw-clipboard-card=, with its backdrop switched off, since the world
drew it).

The backend-blind ones are in [[file:../poc/overlays.lisp][poc/overlays.lisp]] — [[file:../poc/overlays.lisp::defun open-spotter][=open-spotter=]], [[file:../poc/overlays.lisp::defun run-chooser][=run-chooser=]],
[[file:../poc/overlays.lisp::defun run-clipboard-manager][=run-clipboard-manager=]], [[file:../poc/overlays.lisp::defun run-value-prompt][=run-value-prompt=]]; the debugger, its source viewer, the
ASK view and the cheatsheet stay in fb-browser.lisp beside their drawing
([[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun %run-debugger-fb][=%run-debugger-fb=]], [[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun open-frame-source][=open-frame-source=]], [[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun run-agent-view][=run-agent-view=]], [[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun open-cheatsheet][=open-cheatsheet=]]).

*Two ways to be opened.* The plan said "the overlays become morphs instead of
blocking loops", but most of them answer a /caller/ that wants a return value —
=choose-and-run= wants the pick, =set-field= the typed text — and the debugger must
keep the erroring stack standing under it. Rewriting every caller as a callback was
not the step; so:

- =open-overlay= puts it up and returns at once: the Spotter, the cheatsheet, the
  source viewer. Nobody waits; what they do (open a trail) they do themselves.
- [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun run-overlay][=run-overlay=]] puts it up and *waits*, by turning the backend's own loop —
  [[file:../initramfs/fb-browser.lisp::defun fb-cycle][=fb-cycle=]], the body of =run-browser-fb='s loop made a function and installed as
  =*world-cycle*= — until the overlay closes. The same loop, not a copy: so a terminal
  keeps printing behind a menu, the clock ticks behind the debugger, a web page's
  frames arrive. (Squeak's modal dialogs wait the same way, a loop of =doOneCycle=
  until the dialog is gone — from the literature, not checked against an image.) A
  restart invoked from the debugger unwinds straight through that wait; the
  overlay goes on the way out (=unwind-protect=).

*What does stop behind a waiting overlay* — decided by default, pending the user's
review: stepping (step 5) and the agent's tick. The computation that waits may be
the very one a step would run again (a stage whose contact raised the debugger would
raise it again every 33 ms; the old loop froze the stage for the same reason), and
the agent's tick runs tool calls on this thread. Input, the services, repair and the
clock go on.

*A wait opened inside a window* turns the whole loop from inside that window's
dynamic context, where the shell's hit-map specials are bound to the /window's/
values. [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun call-at-top-level][=call-at-top-level=]] sets those bindings aside for the wait (back to the
global values, =sb-ext:symbol-global-value=) and writes whatever the shell recorded
meanwhile back to the top level before the window's come back; the host test checks
that both sets of hit maps survive a menu opened in a window.

*Kept as it was:* every key and click each loop understood; the rule that a modal
never read a key the console did not see (a Ctrl chord or Shift-PageUp that came
only from the keyboard's evdev — those events are now marked =:evdev= and ignored by
overlays, =overlay-key=); the left button only, where a loop read only it; the
=*chooser-state*= and =*dbg-state*= the checks read; the halo excludes the overlays
from its layers (it is one itself).

*Damage.* An overlay damages its whole bounds when what it shows changes — the same
cost as the old full frame — except that moving the selection in a menu, the Spotter
or the clipboard manager damages only the card (=card-damage-rect=, the drop shadow
included), and the value prompt is a band along the bottom (opaque, so the repair
starts at it). The fast paths that paint straight into the canvas (the clock, a
terminal's rows, a web frame) no longer send their rect themselves: they hand it to
the world as /painted/ ([[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun note-painted][=note-painted=]], [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun shell-painted][=shell-painted=]]), and the next repair
draws only what lies above the shell in it — a window's backing store, an overlay's
scrim and card — /before/ the damaged rects, since a rect drawn whole and then
composited again would be dimmed twice. That replaced step 2's =recomposite-windows=,
which knew only windows.

*Not a finding, recorded so nobody repeats it:* the first guest run failed "walking a
real halo OUT ends on the shell morph" — the walk wrapped past the outermost layer.
The probe counted =halo-layers= /while the halo was open/, and the open halo, an
overlay, was then itself a morph under the pointer: one layer more than the real
halo had. Overlays are now left out of the halo's layers. And "a key repairs the
shell … as one full-screen rect" failed once because a clock tick, repaired as
painted-only, overwrote =world-last-repair=; painted-only repairs no longer touch it
(nor =world-repairs=), since both describe repairs of damage.

** Step 5 — stepping: BUILT (2026-10-16)

The world keeps a deadline per stepping morph ([[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun world-step][=world-step=]], [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun world-step-timeout][=world-step-timeout=]]);
=morph-step-ms= is read afresh every turn, so a morph answers NIL the moment it has
nothing to animate and the poll's deadline is -1 again — sleep until input. The
shell morph and each window /hold/ a pane (=holder-pane=) and step when it changes on
its own:

| what                         | step              | damages                                                     |
|------------------------------+-------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------|
| a running stage              | 33 ms             | the stage's body only (=*stage-body-rect*=: viewport, heads-up line, transcript); the tabs, breadcrumbs and status line are not drawn again — unless the step raised the debugger or opened the Workspace beside it |
| a run pane, live or behind   | 100 ms            | its holder; a run in a hidden tab costs nothing             |
| a terminal's rows            | (its pty wakes the poll) | the rows: on the screen for the tiled terminal (painted), in the backing store for a window's, damaged rect by rect — so a terminal in a window no longer redraws whole on each wake |
| a live worker's view         | (its pipe)        | the morph that shows the pane (=damage-pane=), not the shell and every live window |
| the agent's ASK view         | 66 ms while answering, 500 idle | itself (an overlay that steps)                   |

=tick-live-stage= became [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun step-stage-pane][=step-stage-pane=]], for any pane on screen rather than
only the focused one, with each stage's own last-tick time; =runs-timeout= now wakes
only for a stop awaiting its SIGKILL, the panes being stepped by whoever shows them
(runs.lisp, [[file:../initramfs/runs.lisp::defun run-pane-step-ms][=run-pane-step-ms=]], [[file:../initramfs/runs.lisp::defun step-run-panes][=step-run-panes=]]); the live workers' "visible" is
every trail on screen, windows included (=visible-trails=). What stays coarse: the
agent's tick and a service's =:redraw= still call =environment-changed= (the shell
and every live window), and a web page's frame in a /window/ still redraws that
window whole — the web service paints at the shell's geometry.

*Measured* (guest: QEMU with KVM on the laptop's i7-1065G7, =-cpu qemu64=, 1280×800,
averaged over 50 runs because the guest's clock reads in 4 ms ticks):

| what                                                     | guest      |
|----------------------------------------------------------+------------|
| a stage step's repair, body only, drawn and sent          | 2.96 ms    |
| the same step as a full repair (what step 2 did)          | 3.44 ms    |
| loop turns in 5 idle seconds (the clock's ticks, a hide)  | 7          |
| stage steps in 2 s                                        | 55 (the 33 ms period, less what a repair costs) |

The body-only repair saves less than its share of pixels: it still lays out the whole
shell (=draw-browser= under a narrow clip), and that layout is most of the cost — the
same price the status line pays (step 2). Drawing only the stage body when the clip
lies inside it is possible and is owed; the tests' incremental-equals-full rule is
what would make it safe.

* 11. Before and after: one view, built both ways

The same view, the clipboard manager (=SuperL-h=), as it was before Morphic and as it
is now. Nothing about how it /looks/ changed — [[file:../poc/shell.lisp::defun draw-clipboard-card][=draw-clipboard-card=]] draws the card
in both — only who calls that drawing, when, and for which rectangle.

** Before: a view was a function with its own loop

=run-clipboard-fb= in =initramfs/fb-browser.lisp=, as it stood at commit 198fe92
(=git show 198fe92:initramfs/fb-browser.lisp=), abridged:

#+begin_src lisp
(loop with dirty = t do
  (when dirty
    (draw-clipboard-card canvas w h query results sel)   ; repaint the whole screen
    (show-frame canvas)                                  ; send all of it to /dev/fb0
    (setf dirty nil))
  (fbb-wait 0 *fb-mouse* *fb-kbd*)                       ; its own poll(2)
  (cond ((eq key :escape) (return)) ...))                ; its own keys, its own clicks
#+end_src

The view owned the poll, the input decoding and the redraw. While it was open the
rest of the system stood still: a terminal's output, a web page's frames, the stage
and the clock all waited, because only this loop was running. Any change set =dirty=
and the whole screen was drawn and sent again. =run-browser-fb='s own loop and the six
other modal loops (§1's table) each repeated this pattern.

** After: a view is an object, and the World runs the one loop

[[file:../poc/overlays.lisp::defclass clipboard-overlay][=clipboard-overlay=]] in =poc/overlays.lisp=, abridged:

#+begin_src lisp
(defclass clipboard-overlay (overlay) (query sel))

(defmethod overlay-draw ((o clipboard-overlay) canvas w h)   ; how to draw it
  (draw-clipboard-card canvas w h (clip-query o) ...))

(defmethod overlay-event ((o clipboard-overlay) e)           ; what an event does
  (cond ((eq key :escape) (close-overlay o))
        ((eq key :down) (incf (clip-sel o))
                        (overlay-changed o (card-damage-rect *clip-rect*))) ...))

(run-overlay (make-instance 'clipboard-overlay))             ; into the World; wait for the pick
#+end_src

The view says only how to draw itself and what an event does to it. The World does
the rest: one poll, each event to the morph on top ([[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun world-dispatch][=world-dispatch=]]), and the
repair. [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defun run-overlay][=run-overlay=]] waits for the answer by turning that same loop
(=*world-cycle*=), so everything behind the card keeps running. A change is reported
as a rectangle ([[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun invalidate][=invalidate=]]; here only the card); [[file:../poc/morph.lisp::defun world-repair][=world-repair=]] redraws just
that rectangle under a clip, drawing only the morphs that overlap it — and none
beneath the topmost opaque one that covers it — and hands the backend just those
rectangles to send.

** Side by side

| | Before | After |
|---+--------+-------|
| a view is | a function with a loop | a CLOS object (a morph) |
| event loops | 1 main + 6 modal | 1, the World's |
| a change means | =dirty=: draw and send the whole screen | =invalidate= a rect: draw and send that rect |
| an open modal | everything else freezes | everything else keeps running (except stepping and the agent's tick, §10 step 4) |
| overlap | none: a fixed paint order | z-order: windows and overlays are the World's children |
| things that move on their own | each one a special case in the main loop | =step-morph= on a per-morph deadline; idle sleeps |
| the tiled browser | the whole program | one opaque morph, =shell-morph=, unchanged inside |

** What this comparison leaves out

- *Windows.* A [[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defclass trail-window][=trail-window=]] draws into its own backing store, so moving it
  is a copy, not a redraw (§10, step 2). An overlay draws straight onto the screen.
- *Inside the tiled shell nothing is a morph yet.* Its panes, lists and text views are
  still plain structs drawn by =draw-browser=, so a key in the shell still redraws
  the whole shell. Only the stage body and the terminal's rows are finer (§10, step 5).
  Making a pane its own morph is the next step for damage, and the protocol does not
  need to change for it.
- *The Hand.* It carries a window during Move/Resize, and the one drop it knows is a
  window let go over the tab strip, which re-tiles it ([[file:../poc/morphic.lisp::defmethod lol.morph:drop-morph][=drop-morph=]]). Nothing
  else can be dropped /into/ a morph yet — no dragging a value from one view to another.

* 12. Panes as morphs: one abstraction

*STATUS (2026-10-16): DESIGN*, written before the code, at the user's request: "make
each pane its own morph, we should end up with a single abstraction, not having two
ways of doing it." Where a choice was genuinely open it is taken the way most
consistent with §9 and marked /decided by default, pending the user's review/.

** 12.1 The two ways, as they stand

Steps 1–5 put a Morphic world /around/ the browser and left its inside alone (§11, "what
this comparison leaves out"). So there are two ways to be on screen:

1. *The world's way*: a CLOS object with bounds that draws itself and takes events in
   a tree — the desk, the shell morph, a trail window, an overlay.
2. *The shell's old way*, inside =shell-morph=: plain structs (=PANE=) drawn by one
   function (=draw-browser= → =draw-tabs=, =draw-trail-area=, =draw-status-line=) that
   lays everything out from fixed offsets and leaves what it drew where in a score of
   per-frame specials — =*tab-rects*=, =*crumb-rects*=, =*content-top*=, =*split-x*=,
   =*list-rows*=, =*side-rows*=, =*matrix-cells*=, =*stage-rect*=, =*tray-rect*=,
   =*web-page-rect*= … — which the click handlers (=browser-click=, =pointer-target=) and
   the halo (=note-drawn= into =*drawn*=) read back.

The second way leaks into the first wherever they meet. A window can only show a trail
by drawing it into a canvas of its own with its /own copy/ of those specials
(=with-window-context=, a =progv= over =+window-specials+=); a menu opened inside a window
must set those copies aside (=call-at-top-level=); a click in a window is translated to
window coordinates (=local-event=) so that =browser-click= never learns where it is; and
damage inside the shell is all-or-nothing, because nothing smaller than the shell knows
it is on screen: a key in a tiled pane redraws the tab strip, every breadcrumb and the
status line too.

** 12.2 The end state: everything on screen is a morph

#+begin_example
lol.morph (poc/morph.lisp)           CL-USER (poc/shell.lisp, poc/morphic.lisp)
morph ──┬─ world ─────────────────── desk                    the screen
        ├─ hand
        ├─ surface  (new) ─────────── trail-window          a window: draws into its own canvas
        └──────────────────────────┬─ pane                  ← was (defstruct pane)
                                   ├─ shell-morph           the tiled shell: fixed bands
                                   ├─ tab-strip
                                   ├─ tray                  (a child of the tab strip)
                                   ├─ status-line           the status line / the minibuffer
                                   ├─ trail-area            ONE trail, laid out as the accordion
                                   ├─ title-bar             a window's
                                   ├─ window-frame          a window's outline and resize grip
                                   └─ overlay + subclasses  unchanged (step 4)

desk
├── shell-morph                  bounds = the screen
│   ├── tab-strip                    (0 0 w bar)            tabs, +
│   │   └── tray                     right end              clipboard icon, battery, clock
│   ├── trail-area                   between strip and status line
│   │   ├── pane  (collapsed)        a breadcrumb bar, per ancestor
│   │   ├── pane  (collapsed)
│   │   └── pane  (expanded)         the panel and its body
│   │       └── pane  (side)         the pinned side pane, or the nav split's preview
│   └── status-line                  (0 h-s w s)
├── trail-window  (a surface)
│   ├── title-bar
│   ├── trail-area                   the SAME class, laying out the same panes
│   │   └── pane …
│   └── window-frame                 drawn last: the outline and the grip over the pane
└── overlay …                    the Spotter, a menu, the debugger
#+end_example

*=PANE= is the morph.* =(defstruct pane …)= becomes =(defclass pane (lol.morph:morph) …)=
with the same slot names and accessors (=pane-subject=, =pane-view=, =pane-sel=, =pane-top=,
=pane-tv=, =pane-side= …, and =make-pane= / =pane-p= as functions), so =*trails*= is still a
list of lists of panes, every caller is unchanged and the net-REPL-reading checks still
read =(pane-top (current-pane))=. The morph's =subject= slot and the pane's are /one/ slot
(a CLOS slot specifier of the same name merges), so there is no wrapper beside the
struct and no second record of what a pane shows. A pane on screen is a child of a
trail area; a pane in a hidden tab has no owner, draws nothing and costs nothing. What
the specials held becomes the pane's own slots, written when it draws and read when it
takes an event — in its own coordinates:

| was a special                         | is now                                                        |
|---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| =*crumb-rects*=                         | each ancestor /is/ a collapsed pane morph: its bounds         |
| =*content-top*=, =*split-x*=              | =pane-body-top=, =pane-split-x=                                 |
| =*list-rows*=, =*side-rows*=              | =pane-rows=: the main pane's, and the side pane's /own/          |
| =*matrix-cells*=, =*stage-rect*=, =*stage-body-rect*= | =pane-cells=, =pane-stage-rect=, =pane-stage-body=      |
| =*preview-pane*=, =*preview-subject*=     | =pane-preview= (a pane, the side child), per list pane        |
| =*editing-side*=                        | =pane-editing-side= — decided by default (below)               |
| =*tab-rects*=, =*tab-close-rects*=, =*tab-plus-rect*= | slots of the =tab-strip=                              |
| =*tray-rect*=, =*tray-clip-rect*=         | the =tray='s bounds, and a slot                                 |
| =*mb-clear-rect*=                       | a slot of the =status-line=                                     |
| =*web-page-rect*=, =*web-bar-rects*=, =*web-bar-y*= | slots of the =WEB-TAB= the pane shows (web.lisp)       |
| =*drawn*=, =note-drawn=                   | gone: the halo walks the morph tree (§12.5)                   |
| =+window-specials+=, =with-window-context=, =*window-context*=, =*shell-specials*=, =local-event=, =window-chooser=, =offset-layer= | gone: nothing is shared any more, so nothing needs a copy |
| =*status-pane*=, =draw-shell-now=, =render-window=, =draw-trail-area= | gone: each morph draws itself                 |

What stays global is what there is one of. The /hand's/ drag state — =*drag-tv*=,
=*drag-origin*=, =*last-press*= (double-click counting), =*term-drag*=, =*term-pointer*=,
=*web-press*= — describes the one press in progress, and the press and its drag go to the
same morph (the hand's capture), so its coordinates are that morph's.

*Decided by default, pending the user's review — =*editing-side*= becomes per pane.* It
says "the keyboard is in the right-hand column", and it was one special for the whole
screen, which is why every window had to keep its own copy. As a slot of the pane it
says it /about that pane/: switching tabs no longer carries a side focus from one tab to
another (it used to, harmlessly, since every use was guarded by =(pane-side p)=).
=*editing-side*= stays readable and settable as a symbol macro over the current pane, so
=agent-ui.lisp= and =check-agent.lisp= read as before.

** 12.3 Layout is a policy of a container; drawing is per morph

The geometry =draw-trail-area= computed in passing — where each breadcrumb sits, where
the panel starts, where the split falls — moves into a *layout pass* that runs before
every repair and after every event (=desk-sync=). It asks the trails what should be on
screen and makes the tree agree: a =trail-area= takes its trail's panes as children —
ancestors /collapsed/ to a bar each, the last one /expanded/ to the rest of its height —
and the expanded pane takes its side pane or its preview as a child in the right-hand
column (the four cases of the old =cond=: a text pane with a list beside, the stage with
its Workspace, a pinned side pane, the nav-split preview). A morph that is added,
removed or given new bounds is damaged by =add-morph= / =remove-morph= / =set-morph-bounds=
as it already is. Tiling is that one policy; the =shell-morph= puts its trail area
between the tab strip and the status line, a window puts the same class under its
title bar. Nothing else changes layout.

Each morph then draws only itself, at its bounds, with the functions the old
monolith called — =draw-tabs=, =draw-tray=, =draw-crumb=, =draw-panel= + =draw-pane-body=,
=draw-status-line= — split, not rewritten, and back to front in the same order, so a full
repair is the old frame byte for byte (checked as step 0 checked, §12.7). A pane draws
with =*active*= bound to /its/ trail, as =render-window= did, so the code inside that asks
for the current pane still gets its own. =draw-browser= stays, as a name tests and
probes use, and is now only "repair the whole world".

** 12.4 Windows are surfaces holding the same morphs

A *surface* (new in =lol.morph=) is a morph that draws its subtree into a canvas of its
own — the backing store step 2 gave windows, now general: the world's repair stops at a
surface, which repairs its own damaged rects into its canvas (the same back-to-front,
from-the-opaque-cover walk, in its own coordinates) and is then copied to the screen.
Its descendants draw and take events in /surface coordinates/ — the window's top-left is
their 0,0 — while their bounds stay in world coordinates (§3.1's rule): =morph-at= and
damage still compare plain rectangles, and moving a window offsets its subtree's bounds
and changes nothing in its canvas or in any slot recorded in it. That is why a moved
window is still a copy, never a redraw — the step-2 economy, now from the general rule.

A =trail-window= is a surface with three children: a =title-bar=, a =trail-area= and a
=window-frame= (the outline and the grip, drawn last, over the pane). *Tearing off* a
trail makes a window whose trail area lays out that trail: its pane morphs leave the
shell's trail area for the window's (=add-morph= reparents). *Re-tiling* moves them
back. No trail is ever drawn "in a different context" again.

=invalidate= on a morph inside a surface damages its rect on screen /and/ in the
surface's canvas, so a key in a window's pane redraws that pane in the backing store
and copies that rect — not the whole window.

** 12.5 Events go to the deepest morph; the halo walks the tree

=world-dispatch= already sends a key to the focus and a press to the topmost morph under
the point, bubbling up the owners. Two additions make that the /only/ hit testing:

- *Each morph receives events in its own surface's coordinates.* The event carries its
  world point too, for what is placed on the screen (a menu card, the halo).
- *=morph-hit-p=* (default: the bounds) lets a morph be under the point in less than its
  bounds — the window frame only at its grip.

So the click that =browser-click= dispatched by testing a dozen hit maps in turn becomes
the method of the morph it lands on: a tab or the =+= (the tab strip), the clipboard icon
(the tray), the minibuffer's clear box (the status line), a breadcrumb (a collapsed
pane: back to that level), the title bar, the grip — and everything inside an expanded
pane (=pane-click=: the old function's body with the pane's slots for the specials). A
side pane lets a click bubble to its expanded owner, which knows the split, exactly as
=browser-click= treated the right-hand column. A press in a gutter (between breadcrumbs,
beside the panel, on the status line) reaches the trail area or the shell, which hand
it to the expanded pane as the old code did, so no click changes meaning. The focus
change a press makes (=*active*= to that trail, a window raised) is the desk's, before
routing, as the shell morph and the window each did for themselves.

*The keyboard focus is the expanded pane* of the focused trail — in the shell or in a
window — and =browser-key= is its =handle-event=. The desk still sees every key first
(=SuperL-g= and the grab mode).

*The halo* is the morph under the point and its owners: =halo-layers= becomes the target
under the pointer (the word, the row, the sprite — =target-layers= as before), then for
each morph from the deepest out to the shell or the window, the layers that morph
offers: a pane its body (the text view, the list, the matrix, the stage, the page, the
terminal) when the point is on it, then the pane itself, then what it shows; a
breadcrumb itself; the tab strip a tab and itself; the tray the clipboard icon and
itself; the status line the minibuffer and itself; a window its title bar and itself.
=note-drawn= and =*drawn*= go: there is nothing left that is drawn but is not a morph.
/Decided by default:/ the old outermost element, "the screen" (the canvas, Code:
=draw-browser=), goes with them — the shell morph and the window are now the outermost
things, and their =Code= handles open the drawing that is really theirs.

** 12.6 Damage per morph

- *An event damages the morph it reached*: a key in a pane, that pane (and its side
  column, its child); a click, the pane clicked.
- *Every other change is found, not announced.* Each chrome morph has a /signature/ —
  the few values its picture is a function of: the tab strip's titles, tints, numbers,
  agent dots and focused tab; the tray's items (the clock aside — it ticks on its own, as
  now); the status line's text, minibuffer or hints; a breadcrumb's title and pick; a
  window's title and focus. A pane's signature is its view, side, preview, selection,
  side focus and its text's buffer and point (the buffer is a persistent vector, so "did
  it change?" is one =EQ=), plus what its subject adds (a web tab's address, load state
  and title). The layout pass compares each morph's signature with the one it had and
  damages only the morphs whose picture changed. So the agent streaming into a
  conversation damages that conversation's pane, a web page's title its pane and its
  tab, a file saved in one folder pane the other folder panes that show it — and no
  =environment-changed= has to guess.
- *The fast paths paint into whichever surface the pane is in*: a terminal's dirty rows
  and a web page's frame go straight onto the screen for a tiled pane (=note-painted=, as
  now) and into the backing store for a window's, with only those rects damaged — so a
  web page in a window no longer redraws the window per frame, and gets its hover and
  wheel at the right place.
- *Stepping belongs to the pane.* A running stage steps itself and damages its body
  rect, and the repair draws only that pane (the panel under a clip, the stage body) —
  not the tab strip, the breadcrumbs or the status line, which no longer lie under any
  "shell" draw. A run pane steps itself and damages itself.
- *World-wide damage only for what is world-wide*: the font size (every metric), the
  screen's geometry, a new world.

** 12.7 Migration, each step leaving every check green

1. *The core* (=poc/morph.lisp=): surfaces, events in surface coordinates with their
   world point, =morph-hit-p=, repair that stops at a surface, =world-paint= (draw the
   whole tree once). A host test of its own; nothing in the browser changes.
2. *=PANE= becomes a CLOS morph*, and the pane-scoped specials become its slots (and the
   web tab's). The monolithic draw still runs; =+window-specials+= shrinks to the
   chrome's.
3. *The tiled shell becomes a tree*: tab strip, tray, trail area, panes, status line;
   the layout pass; per-morph drawing and routing; the halo from the tree; =note-drawn=,
   =*drawn*= and the chrome's specials gone; =draw-browser= = a full repair. Damage stays
   whole-shell in this step.
4. *Windows become surfaces* holding a trail area of the same pane morphs; tear-off and
   re-tile move morphs; =with-window-context= and its machinery, =render-window=,
   =draw-trail-area= and =call-at-top-level='s juggling gone.
5. *Damage per morph*: event damage, signatures, the fast paths into any surface, panes
   that step; =environment-changed= reduced to what is truly global.
6. Tests and docs: =poc/morphic-test.lisp= keeps "incremental repair = full repair, pixel
   for pixel, after every step" and adds per-pane damage (a key in a pane repairs that
   pane and, if it changed, the status line); =render-frame.lisp= frames before/after;
   =check-morphic.lisp= extended; the guest measured against §10's numbers.
