#+TITLE: Screencasts — recording the environment, with sound, from inside it
#+STARTUP: showall

A screencast of the environment, with a voice explaining it, is made *from inside the
environment*: a key starts recording, you talk, pause, resume, stop, watch the encode,
play the result back full screen, and only then hand the file on.

*STATUS: built.* Kernel tag #29 (sound), =/dev/snd= for the user, the mixer and volume
keys ([[file:../initramfs/mixer.lisp][mixer.lisp]]), the recorder in two modes — live and damage log
([[file:../initramfs/recorder.lisp][recorder.lisp]], [[file:../initramfs/damage-log.lisp][damage-log.lisp]]) — and the full-screen player ([[file:../initramfs/player.lisp][player.lisp]]). Tested in
QEMU by =check-sound.lisp= and =check-recorder.lisp= (39 checks), and on a Dell XPS 13
9300 with a live take and playback, with sound ([[#measured][Measured]]). Not yet tried on real hardware: a
logged take, a long take, the clap test for sync, a headset through the jack ([[#open-items-risks][Open items and risks]]).

Sound is a requirement, so this is kernel work as well as userland: tag #28 had no
sound (=# CONFIG_SOUND is not set=); tag #29 adds it ([[file:kernel-config.org::#sound-new-tag-29][kernel-config.org, Sound — NEW in tag #29]], snapshot
[[file:../kernel/config-7.2.5][kernel/config-7.2.5]]).

* 1. The shape

#+begin_example
   PID 1 (the environment's thread)            container "default" (the host's Debian)
   ───────────────────────────────            ─────────────────────────────────────────
   world-repair ─► present-repair ──► /dev/fb0
                        │
                        └─ tap: "the canvas changed"
                                │
   sampler (30 fps, only a copy │                ┌──────────────── ffmpeg ─────────────────┐
   of the canvas + the pointer) ▼                │ in 0: rawvideo bgr0 on stdin            │
   bounded queue ─► writer thread ─── pipe ────► │ in 1: -f alsa plughw:0,0  (the mic)     │
                                                 │ out : seg-NNN.mp4 (H.264 + AAC)         │
   recorder pane / tray ◄── meter thread ◄─ FIFO │ out : 8 kHz mono s16 PCM (the meter)    │
                                                 └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
   stop ─► ffmpeg -f concat -c copy ─► NAME.mp4 ─► ffprobe -of json ─► result.lisp
   play ─► *screen-lent* ─► the host's mplayer -vo fbdev2 -slave (keys ─► its stdin) ─► hand back
#+end_example

Two ideas carry it:

- *The pixels are already in Lisp memory.* The environment draws into a canvas and
  copies rects of it to the framebuffer, so recording is "copy the canvas sometimes",
  not "read the screen back".
- *The codecs are the host's.* ffmpeg, MPlayer and libasound run in the default
  container ([[file:containers.org][containers.org]]) — the same rule as OpenSSL and Chrome: host programs are
  used at runtime from =/mnt/host=, never baked into the image. The image grows by a
  few hundred lines of Lisp and a handful of kernel options.

* 2. The sound hardware
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sound-hardware
:END:

Tested on a Dell XPS 13 9300 (i7-1065G7):

- *Controller.* =00:1f.3 Intel Ice Lake-LP Smart Sound Technology Audio Controller
  [8086:34c8]=. Three drivers can bind it (=snd_soc_avs=, =snd_sof_pci_intel_icl=,
  =snd_hda_intel=); Debian's kernel picks =snd_hda_intel= (=snd_intel_dspcfg.dsp_driver=
  0, "decide by the hardware").
- *Codecs.* =Realtek ALC289= (codec 0) and =Intel Icelake HDMI= (codec 2).
- *The mics are analogue, on the codec.* Node 0x12 is the built-in mic ("Internal Mic
  Boost Volume"); 0x19 and 0x1b are the headset jack's mics.
- *PCMs.* =00-00: ALC289 Analog : playback 1 : capture 1=, plus four HDMI playbacks.

*Why it matters.* On many Intel laptops the built-in mics are /digital/ (DMIC) and wired
to the DSP; then only SOF works, which needs the DSP's firmware and a topology file
copied from the host like the Wi-Fi firmware ([[file:networking.org::#wi-fi][networking.org, Wi-Fi]]). Here the mic goes
through the Realtek codec, which plain HD-Audio drives with no firmware. So we use the
*legacy HDA path*, and SOF isn't even built, so =snd_intel_dspcfg= can't pick it.

*Always name the card.* The container's libasound reads the host's ALSA config, and a
Debian with PipeWire has =/etc/alsa/conf.d/99-pipewire-default.conf= pointing ALSA's
=default= at PipeWire. There's no PipeWire in the guest, so every command line names
=plughw:0,0= (=plug= converts the hardware's format and rate into what's asked; bare
=hw:= would refuse a mismatch).

** 2.1 Choosing the mic: built-in or headset
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: choosing-mic-built-headset
:END:

A wired headset on the 3.5 mm combo jack (one 4-pole plug for earphones and mic) is the
better narration mic; the built-in one is the fallback. Same codec, same driver: node
0x19 is the headset mic ("Headset Mic Boost Volume"), 0x1b the "Headphone Mic".
Nothing extra goes in the kernel for it.

- *Headset detection is in the kernel.* =alc269.c= has the Dell chain
  =ALC269_FIXUP_DELL4_MIC_NO_PRESENCE= → =ALC269_FIXUP_HEADSET_MODE=, which declares 0x19
  the headset mic "without its own jack detect" and lets the codec's headset mode tell
  a headset from plain earphones. On Debian, PipeWire only reads that state.
- *Jack controls.* The HDA driver exposes each jack as a boolean "… Jack" control. On
  the ALC289 that's =Headphone Mic Jack= (the combo jack's sense: =1= with a plug in)
  and three /phantom/ jacks, =Internal Mic Phantom Jack=, =Headset Mic Phantom Jack=,
  =Speaker Phantom Jack=, always =1= because they stand for fixed devices.
- *The input is an enumerated control.* =Capture Source= picks it; its items are
  "Internal Mic", "Headset Mic", ….
- *What the image does.* =choose-mic= picks "Headset Mic" when a mic jack senses a
  plug, else "Internal Mic", at boot and when a recording starts. =M= in the recorder
  pane (=next-mic=) picks the next input by hand, for a headset whose jack isn't sensed.

*Ruled out: Bluetooth headsets.* A Bluetooth mic needs the kernel's Bluetooth stack and
the AX201's Bluetooth firmware, the host's =bluetoothd= with a D-Bus system bus, and
*PipeWire* in the guest, since the mic only exists in the hands-free profile (HFP),
which BlueZ alone doesn't implement. HFP audio is also phone quality (8 kHz, or 16 kHz
with mSBC). *USB headsets* would be one kernel option (=SND_USB_AUDIO=, no firmware)
and a card picker in the preflight; not built.

** 2.2 The speakers

=alc269.c= has a Dell fixup for this family (=ALC289_FIXUP_DELL_SPK1=), applied by the
codec's PCI subsystem id (Dell 096d). On the first boot the codec reported one speaker
line out (0x17) and one headphone out (0x21) ([[#measured][Measured]]).

** 3.1 Userland for the card: device nodes and the mixer
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: userland-card-device-nodes
:END:

- *Device nodes.* =give_devices= calls =chown_dir_matching("/dev/snd", "", uid, gid)=,
  which skips non-character entries, so =by-path/= is left alone.
- *Why a mixer of our own.* On Debian, WirePlumber and =alsactl= set the codec's
  switches and gains at boot; the guest has neither. The ALC289's boot defaults are
  *mute* for playback and the mic (the first boot's log shows =restore-audio= switching
  on =Master Playback Switch= and =Capture Switch=), so this job isn't optional.
- *An ALSA control.* A numbered element with a name, a type and a range — what
  =alsamixer= shows. [[file:../initramfs/mixer.lisp][mixer.lisp]] reaches them with four ioctls on
  =/dev/snd/controlC0= (=SNDRV_CTL_IOCTL_ELEM_LIST= / =ELEM_INFO= / =ELEM_READ= /
  =ELEM_WRITE=), in the style of =net.lisp='s ioctls. Enumerated controls are u32 items,
  not the volumes' longs.
- *=restore-audio= at boot.* Every playback and capture switch on; Master at the saved
  =:volume= / =:muted= from =~/.lol/settings= (default 50 %); the capture gain in dB; the
  mic chosen ([[#choosing-mic-built-headset][Choosing the mic: built-in or headset]]). Its =sound:= line in =session.log= lists what it switched on.
- *The capture gain is set in dB, not raised to the top.* =Capture Volume='s TLV (the
  dB scale the driver publishes, read with =SNDRV_CTL_IOCTL_TLV_READ=) runs from -17.25
  dB in 0.75 dB steps, so its top (63) is +30 dB — enough to clip speech ([[#measured][Measured]]).
  =set-capture-db= sets the saved =:capture-db=, else =*default-capture-db*= = +21.75 dB
  (step 52, where Debian's PipeWire keeps it).
- *Volume keys.* =audio-key= serves volume up / down / mute / mic-mute, read from the
  keyboard's evdev (=KEY_VOLUMEUP= 115, =KEY_VOLUMEDOWN= 114, =KEY_MUTE= 113,
  =KEY_MICMUTE= 248; no tty byte), so they work everywhere in the environment, during
  playback too. They step 5 % (=*volume-step*=); =*volume-intent*= remembers the level
  it set so steps don't drift through the codec's raw levels (0–87 on Master).
- *The tray.* =sound-tray-item= is =VOL 50%= or =VOL mute=.

*Why not =pactl=?* =pactl= is a client of a sound /server/ (PipeWire speaking the
PulseAudio protocol), which then writes these same ALSA controls. The image runs no
sound server, so =pactl= would find nobody to talk to; =mixer.lisp= writes the
controls directly, which is where both tools end.

* 4. Recording: the frames
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: recording-frames
:END:

** 4.1 The tap

=present-repair= and =present-frame= call the functions in =*frame-taps*= (a list,
because a screen shared to a web page taps it too: [[file:web-browser.org::#video-calls-google-meet][web-browser.org, Video calls: Google Meet, with screen sharing]]). The tap
gets the repair's rects or "the whole frame". In live mode it only sets a flag.

Copying at the rate of repairs would be wrong both ways: an idle screen makes no
repairs (the video would have holes), a busy one makes more than 30 a second.

** 4.2 The sampler: a constant frame rate

Video files want a constant rate. The sampler is a service (=:timeout= / =:run=, like
the web pane) with a slot every 1/30 s (=*rec-fps*=):

- *Changed slot.* If the canvas was presented or the pointer moved since the last
  copy, copy the canvas into a pooled buffer and draw the arrow onto the copy.
- *Unchanged slot.* Queue the last frame again — a counter, not a copy (the queue
  holds =(buffer . copies)=).
- *Late wake-up.* Every slot that's due gets a frame, so the frame /count/ stays the
  video's clock ([[#live-encoding][Live encoding (the default)]]).

** 4.3 Never stall the environment
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: never-stall-environment
:END:

A recorder that blocks the environment's thread freezes the screen, and the
watchdog's STALL report ([[file:diagnostics.org][diagnostics.org]]) would say so.

- *A writer thread* feeds ffmpeg's stdin; the environment's thread never writes to it.
- *A fixed pool.* Four screen-sized buffers per recording (=*rec-pool-size*=), so the
  GC sees no garbage. When none is free the sampler repeats the last frame — timing
  kept, picture a frame or two stale — and counts it. The result reports *stale*
  frames instead of looking fine and stuttering.
- *Segment ends off-thread.* Pause and stop stop sampling at once (=rec-close-segment=,
  which also counts the time) and finish the segment on a thread (=rec-in-background=),
  in state =:finishing= meanwhile.

** 4.4 What appears in the frame

Everything on screen, including the pointer and the tray's REC indicator. The
recorder's pane shows if it's open; the usual move is =SuperL-r= from wherever you are,
opening the pane only after stopping.

* 5. Recording to a file
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: recording-file
:END:

** 5.1 Live encoding (the default)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: live-encoding
:END:

One ffmpeg per segment ([[#pause-resume-segments][Pause and resume: segments]]), in the default container, from =rec-segment-argv=:

#+begin_example
ffmpeg -hide_banner -loglevel warning -nostats -y \
  -thread_queue_size 64 -f rawvideo -pix_fmt bgr0 -s 1920x1200 -r 30 -i pipe:0 \
  -thread_queue_size 1024 -f alsa -ac 2 -i plughw:0,0 \
  -map 0:v -map 1:a \
    -c:v libx264 -preset ultrafast -crf 23 -pix_fmt yuv420p -g 60 \
    -c:a aac -b:a 160k -ar 48000 \
    -af aresample=async=1 -shortest -movflags +faststart seg-001.mp4 \
  -map 1:a -ac 1 -ar 8000 -f s16le -y /tmp/lol-rec-meter-PID
#+end_example

- *No timestamps.* Frames go in at a constant 30 fps; the count is the clock.
  =aresample=async=1= stretches the sound by a sample now and then, since the card's
  clock and ours differ slightly.
- *The mic meter.* The second output is the mic as 8 kHz mono into a FIFO in the
  shared =/tmp=, read by a meter thread (opened non-blocking, so a FIFO nobody writes
  yet can't hang it). Its level is the bar in the tray and pane. "Was the mic on?" is
  the classic way a screencast is lost; this answers it before ten minutes of talking.

*A/V sync, proved on the host before any recorder code.* A Lisp generator wrote frames
into ffmpeg's stdin at 30 fps and, at the same instant, a 100 ms tone; every 2 s the
frame went white while the tone sounded. ffmpeg recorded the frames (no wall-clock
stamps) and a PipeWire null sink's monitor standing in for the mic. Over 3 minutes all
89 beeps sat *26.7 ms* behind their flash, first and last alike: *no drift*. That's one
frame, under the ~45 ms nobody notices, and part of it is the probe's own playback
buffer.

*Encoder cost* (the XPS 13 9300's Debian, 10 s of 1920×1200 =testsrc2= — noisier than a
UI — piped as raw frames into =libx264 ultrafast=): *4.1× real time, ~0.95
CPU-seconds per recorded second*, about one of the eight hardware threads.

** 5.2 The damage log (the second mode)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: damage-log
:END:

=m= in the recorder pane switches the next recording between =live= and =log=
(=*rec-mode*=); the tray then says =REC LOG=. A logged take records only what changed
and encodes after stop:

- *The picture.* In log mode the tap cuts the repair's rects out of the canvas and
  hands them to a log-writer thread, which compresses them with =lol.deflate=
  ([[file:../poc/deflate.lisp][poc/deflate.lisp]]) and appends them to =seg-NNN.dmg=. The sampler's slots only note
  the pointer when it moved.
- *The format.* [[file:../initramfs/damage-log.lisp][damage-log.lisp]]: magic =LOLDMG1=, the screen's size, then records of
  rects (zlib) or of the pointer, each stamped in µs since the log began.
  =dmg-replay= turns a log into frames at a rate: every record up to frame k's time,
  the pointer drawn on a copy.
- *The sound.* An audio-only ffmpeg (=rec-audio-argv=): the mic into =seg-NNN.flac=
  (lossless) plus the meter FIFO, with =-loglevel info=, its stderr kept as
  =seg-NNN.alog=.
- *Alignment.* Two processes, so the sound's place must be measured. ffmpeg prints each
  input's =start:= when it opens it; for a live device that's the *wall clock* of the
  first sample (=start: 1792339442.244653=, 38 ms after the spawn). The log keeps its
  own wall-clock start; the difference becomes =-itsoffset= at encoding (=alog-start=,
  =rec-encode-log=).
- *After stop.* A thread replays each log at 30 fps into ffmpeg with its FLAC, then the
  segments are joined as a live take's are. =SAVING n%= counts frames replayed over the
  total. Logs and sound files are deleted once encoded.

|                                          | Live ([[#live-encoding][Live encoding (the default)]])        | Damage log ([[#damage-log][The damage log (the second mode)]])                                  |
|------------------------------------------+--------------------+----------------------------------------------------|
| CPU while recording                      | ~1 thread          | the copies of changed rects + deflate; ~0 when idle |
| Wait after stop                          | seconds (a concat) | the whole encode                                   |
| Stale frames under load                  | possible, counted  | none: every repair is in the log                   |
| Re-encode later (other fps, size, codec) | lossy, from the MP4 | exact, from the log                               |

The log is the better recording and the worse experience; live is the reverse. Both
share the sampler, the meter, the segments and the result view.

** 5.3 Pause and resume: segments
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: pause-resume-segments
:END:

ffmpeg can't pause an ALSA input, and a paused recording shouldn't contain the pause.
So *a pause ends a segment* and *resume starts the next* (=seg-002=, …).

- *Ending a segment.* Close stdin, give ffmpeg a second to read the last frames, then
  SIGINT, its graceful stop (flush the encoder, write the index). =-shortest= ends the
  main output, but the meter output is live mic alone and never ends by itself.
  Exit code 255 counts as success *when ffprobe finds a video in the file*.
- *Stop* ends the last segment and joins them, reading the join's =-progress= file for
  =SAVING n%=; a single segment is renamed instead:

  #+begin_example
  ffmpeg -f concat -safe 0 -i segments.txt -c copy -movflags +faststart NAME.mp4
  #+end_example

  =-c copy= re-encodes nothing — the segments share every parameter — so the join takes
  seconds.

** 5.4 Files

A recording is a folder =~/.lol/rec/<stamp>/= (via =lol-path=, mode 0700). While
recording it holds the segments; afterwards =screencast-<stamp>.mp4=, =result.lisp= and
=ffmpeg.log= (stderr, for when it fails). The preflight refuses to start with less than
1 GB free (=*rec-min-free*=).

* 6. The result

=ffprobe -of json= on the finished file, read with =lol.json=, plus the recorder's own
counts, go into =result.lisp= beside the video: duration, size, size in pixels,
codecs, frames written and stale, segments, the take's loudest moment, the mic and its
gain (=:mic=, =:gain=), the mode.

The output is MP4: H.264 =yuv420p= (which every player and browser takes) and AAC 48
kHz, =+faststart= so a web page can play it before it has all of it.

A take whose loudest moment stayed under -50 dB is flagged =(silent!)= in the list and
"silent? check the mic" in its result.

*The pane* (=subj-recorder=, Spotter "record" / "recordings"): state, time, the mic as

| Key       | Does                                                             |

| =M=       | the next mic input (=next-mic=)                                  |
| =+= / =-= | mic gain ±3 dB, remembered as =:capture-db=                      |
| Enter     | a recording's result view                                        |

| =f=       | open its folder in the file manager, the video selected          |
| =D=       | delete it (refused while recording)                              |

The file manager plays videos too: an opened video takes =p= (full screen) and =w= (a
window); its preview is a frame from 10 % in plus ffprobe's facts ([[file:file-manager.org][file-manager.org]]).

** 7.3 Preflight

Checked before anything starts; each failure is the status line's message and the
pane's first row:

1. a sound card — else "this kernel has none (tag #29 or later)";
2. =/dev/snd/pcmC0D0c= openable — else lol-rootd hasn't given =/dev/snd= yet, or the mic
   is busy;
3. a home on a disk, with 1 GB free;
4. containers available, and the host's =/usr/bin/ffmpeg=.

* 8. Playback: the host's player
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: playback-host-s-player
:END:

We don't build a player. Decoding, A/V sync, seeking and audio output are a player's
whole job, and the host has players that do it well; our part is to lend the screen
and pass the keys.

** 8.2 MPlayer full screen, the screen lent
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: mplayer-full-screen-screen
:END:

Only one process may write =/dev/fb0= at a time ([[file:processes.org][processes.org]]): two writers overwrite
each other and nobody owns the result. So playing is a *hand-off*, the way leaving to
the console is. =(play-video FILE)=:

1. *Lend the screen.* =*screen-lent*= turns off all five writers of =/dev/fb0= ([[#builds][What it builds on]]).
   The world keeps running — the tray's clock, a streaming agent reply, a terminal's
   output still repair the canvas; they reach the screen after the hand-back.
2. *Run the host's player* in the default container, where our =/dev/fb0= and
   =/dev/snd= are bind-mounted:

   #+begin_example
   mplayer -slave … -vo fbdev2 -ao alsa:device=plughw=0.0 -vf scale=W:H FILE
   #+end_example

   =-vf scale= to the screen's size (=*fb-xres*= × =*fb-yres*=): fbdev2 has no scaler
   and otherwise /centres/ the clip at its own size.
3. *Keys stay ours.* While the screen is lent the loop gives input events to
   =player-event=, which turns them into slave commands:

   | Key                      | Command                                      |
   |--------------------------+----------------------------------------------|
   | =SPC=                    | =pause= (a toggle)                           |
   | =←= / =→=, =S-←= / =S-→= | =seek -5= / =seek 5=, =seek -30= / =seek 30= |
   | =0=–=9=                  | =seek N0 1= (percent)                        |
   | =o=                      | =osd_show_progression=                       |
   | =q=, =Esc=               | =quit=, then the hand-back                   |

   The volume keys still work: they're read before events reach anyone.
4. *Hand back.* On =q= or the end, MPlayer is quit — and killed if it's still there 3 s
   later — then =*screen-lent*= is cleared and the whole canvas presented once.

=:window t= is the interim alternative: MPlayer's =fbdev= in a terminal tab, drawing a
640-wide window over the right edge ([[#playing-file-hand-checking][Playing a file by hand, and checking the sound]]).

*What the hand-off costs.* Full screen only; no pointer, no overlays while it plays.
For "watch the take before sharing it", that's the right view anyway.

** 8.3 Ruled out
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ruled-out
:END:

- *Our own decoder into a pane.* ffmpeg decoding to =bgra= over a pipe into a
  =video-body= (~400 lines) would let the video sit in a window, but means writing
  pause, seeking and A/V sync — a player. A host player that decodes into our memory is
  libvlc's =vmem=, through a small C helper, since =libvlc-dev= isn't installed and
  libvlc calls back from its own threads, which PID 1 shouldn't host.
- *MPlayer in a window's rect, as a built mode.* =-vo fbdev -geometry X:Y -vf
  scale=W:H= works (=-geometry= is honoured by =fbdev=, not =fbdev2=), but it's two
  writers on one screen again. It would need rules: our presents leave the rect out;
  anything covering it pauses MPlayer; the pointer hides over it; a moved window
  restarts MPlayer at =-ss=; a paused frame is read back into the window's store. Not
  built.
- *VLC's =fb= output.* Works the same way but takes the tty by default (=--fb-tty=);
  MPlayer's fbdev2 does less and its slave protocol is plain text. VLC is the second
  choice.
- *ffplay, mpv.* No framebuffer output / not installed ([[#host-s-players][The host's players]]).
- *Chrome's =<video>= in the web pane.* Its screencast is JPEG at Chrome's chosen rate,
  blurrier and choppier than the file you want to judge.

** 8.4 MPlayer on efifb, tested

In QEMU (OVMF + efifb at 1280×800, MPlayer from a terminal pane): both =fbdev2= (full
screen) and =fbdev= with =-geometry= (a window) draw on efifb's fixed 32-bpp mode with
correct colours; the environment keeps running and our =KD_GRAPHICS= tty is left alone.

- *=fbdev2= centres* the picture without scaling it; =-vf scale= to the screen fills it.
- *=fbdev= doesn't clip.* A window past the right edge wraps onto the left of the next
  line (=1000:150= on a 1280-wide screen). Keep =-geometry= inside the screen.
- *=do_connect: could not connect to socket=* is LIRC, the infrared-remote client;
  =-nolirc= silences it.

** 8.5 Playing a file by hand, and checking the sound
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: playing-file-hand-checking
:END:

Useful to check picture and sound on new hardware without the recorder: MPlayer in a
terminal tab, drawing over the environment. =check-sound.lisp= uses exactly this form.

*A test clip*, in a terminal (=SuperL-Return=) — colour bars, a running clock and a
440 Hz tone, 20 s:

#+begin_example
ffmpeg -f lavfi -i testsrc2=size=640x400:rate=30 -f lavfi -i sine=frequency=440 \
       -t 20 -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac ~/lol-test.mp4
#+end_example

*The =play= helper.* Paste into a Workspace (=SuperL-S-e= opens a new one), put the
caret after the last parenthesis and =C-x C-e= (/Do it/). The status line must end in
~=> PLAY~; otherwise the =defun= wasn't evaluated and =(play …)= reports "undefined
function: PLAY".

#+begin_src lisp
(defun play (file &key window)
  "Play FILE (in your home) with the host's mplayer, in a new tab that takes its keys:
   full screen, or with :WINDOW T a window 640 wide against the right edge."
  ;; PROBE-FILE resolves "~/..." (SBCL reads it as :HOME) and a name relative to your
  ;; home to the real absolute path; NAMESTRING would keep the "~" and mplayer, with no
  ;; shell in between, would not find it. -really-quiet says NOTHING about a missing
  ;; file and exits 0, so the check is ours.
  (let* ((found (probe-file (merge-pathnames file (user-homedir-pathname))))
         (path (if found (sb-ext:native-namestring found) (error "no such file: ~a" file))))
    (open-terminal
     (container-command-line
      (append (list "mplayer" "-really-quiet" "-nolirc" "-ao" "alsa:device=plughw=0.0")
              (if window
                  (list "-vo" "fbdev" "-geometry" "100%:50%" "-vf" "scale=640:-3")   ; -3: keep the aspect
                  (list "-vo" "fbdev2" "-vf" (format nil "scale=~d:~d" *fb-xres* *fb-yres*)))
              (list path)))
     :title "mplayer")))
#+end_src

Then, each with =C-x C-e=:

#+begin_src lisp
(play "lol-test.mp4" :window t)   ; a window, flush right, centred vertically
(play "lol-test.mp4")             ; the whole screen
#+end_src

- *The file.* A name in your home, a =~/= path or an absolute one; a missing file is
  an error in the status line.
- *How it works.* =container-command-line= ([[file:../initramfs/containers.lisp][containers.lisp]]) wraps the argv for the
  default container; =open-terminal= runs it on a pty in a *new tab*
  ([[file:terminal.org][terminal.org]]), so while that tab has focus your keys go to MPlayer.
- *=C-x C-e=, not =C-c C-c=.* Accept would journal =play= and compile it back in on
  every boot ([[file:session-persistence.org][session-persistence.org]]).
- *Keys.* MPlayer's own: =SPC= pause, =←= / =→= 10 s, =↑= / =↓= a minute, =9= / =0= volume,
  =o= clock, =q= quit; then =Return= closes the tab.
- *What you'll see.* The environment still paints over the video (the tray's clock
  every second, a changing pane over a window), and the last frame stays after =q=
  until something redraws it. That's what [[#mplayer-full-screen-screen][MPlayer full screen, the screen lent]]'s hand-off fixes.

The same from a terminal (=-vo fbdev= with =-geometry X:Y=, the window's top-left in
pixels or as a percentage of the room left over):

#+begin_example
mplayer -really-quiet -nolirc -ao alsa:device=plughw=0.0 -vo fbdev \
        -geometry 100%:50% -vf scale=640:400 ~/lol-test.mp4
mplayer -really-quiet -nolirc -ao alsa:device=plughw=0.0 -vo fbdev2 \
        -vf scale=1920:1200 ~/lol-test.mp4
#+end_example

=-ao alsa:device=plughw=0.0= is MPlayer's spelling of =plughw:0,0= — not ALSA's
=default= ([[#sound-hardware][The sound hardware]]).

*If it plays without sound:*

- *No =VOL= in the tray* means no card; the boot's =session.log= (=~/.lol/log=) has a
  =sound:= line when =restore-audio= found one.
- *=VOL mute= or low.* The volume keys fix both; the status line says =Volume 55%=.
- *Headset plugged in* moves the sound from the speakers to it (the kernel's
  =Auto-Mute Mode=). =C-c C-p= on =(getf (find-control "Headphone Mic Jack") :values)=
  prints =(1)= with a plug in.
- *Every control.* =C-c C-p= on =(mixer-controls)=. The ALC289 has =Master=,
  =Headphone= and =Speaker= playback volumes and switches (0–87), =Capture= volume
  (0–63) and switch, =Capture Source=, =Auto-Mute Mode=, three mic boosts (0–3), and
  the jacks of [[#choosing-mic-built-headset][Choosing the mic: built-in or headset]].
- *Volume keys do nothing?* Some laptops' Fn keys arrive on a device other than the AT
  keyboard; SuperL-Space → "input devices" lists them.

* 11. Pitfalls

- *A segment doesn't end by itself.* The meter output is live mic, so =-shortest= alone
  never finishes ffmpeg: close stdin, wait a second, SIGINT; 255 is success if ffprobe
  finds a video ([[#pause-resume-segments][Pause and resume: segments]]).
- *Never end a segment on the environment's thread.* Done there, pause froze the
  screen until ffmpeg's timeout ([[#never-stall-environment][Never stall the environment]]).
- *=-copyts= into Matroska doesn't keep epoch timestamps.* Tried for the log's
  alignment: ffmpeg wrote a file it couldn't read back ("Duplicate element", "End of
  file"). Hence =-itsoffset= from =start:= ([[#damage-log][The damage log (the second mode)]]).
- *A saved =NIL= setting read back as =:NIL=* (true), so a boot after a volume key
  started muted. =settings.lisp= now maps =:nil= / =:t= back.
- *QEMU's home under =LOL_GUEST_HOSTROOT=1= is a tmpfs* with 0.3 GB free; the 1 GB rule
  refuses there, so the check lowers =*rec-min-free*=.
- *=sb-unix= lacks =unix-chmod=, =unix-rmdir= and =o_nonblock=.* Use =files.lisp='s
  =%chmod= / =%rmdir=; =O_NONBLOCK= is =#o4000= (=asm-generic/fcntl.h=).
- *Test traps.* =key-event= builds a QMP event and =qmp-events= sends it (calling only
  the first pressed nothing). A binary PPM's header is P6, width, height, maxval —
  reading the third field as the width made a correct hand-back look wrong.

* 13. Open items and risks
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: open-items-risks
:END:

*Not yet seen on real hardware:*

- the meter moving with a voice (QEMU's capture is silence);
- a logged take's sync next to a live one's — clap or tap a key near the mic while it
  changes the screen — and the log's size on a long take of real work;
- a long take's stale count and CPU (=top= in a terminal);
- the headset through the jack: does =headset-plugged-p= sense it, does a 3-pole
  earphone get mistaken for a headset, and the noise floor of a take through it.

*Not built, each optional:* jack plug/unplug events during a take
(=SNDRV_CTL_IOCTL_SUBSCRIBE_EVENTS= on =controlC0=); an on-screen *key display* (a viewer
can't see =C-c C-c= pressed); *system audio* (=SND_ALOOP= mixed with the mic, so a web
page's sound is in the take); a *trim* (=-ss/-to -c copy=); export to WebM or GIF; a
preflight check that the mic isn't silent.

*Risks:*

- *Battery and heat* on a long live take: ~1 of 8 threads.
- *The combo jack* may misdetect a plug: the mic is silent or the built-in one is
  used. The status line names the mic at start, and the silent flag catches the rest
  after.
- *A slow or full disk.* The writer repeats frames and counts them stale; ffmpeg's
  error goes to =ffmpeg.log=; the environment never waits.
